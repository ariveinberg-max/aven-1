# Build log — Entry 5

Day 15, the first day of Phase 1. A real fix to a bug that's been sitting open since Day 10, a third reward-model/RAFT cycle, and a concrete lead on a parameter-count mystery that's been open since Day 3.

## raft.py's diversity bug, finally fixed
Since Day 10, `raft.py` has had its own candidate-generation temperature schedule (a 0.5–0.9 spread) that never received the fix already applied to the labeling tool's pair-generation endpoint back on Day 9 — a confident model just stops diverging at low temperature, and RAFT's candidate spread had been quietly collapsing (7.895 → 1.726, 16/19 prompts at zero spread) as a result.

Fixed it by porting the proven pattern over directly: uniform 1.3 sampling, plus a per-candidate retry (up to 6 attempts) against a cheap plausibility filter and de-duplication against what's already been generated for that prompt. Measured, not assumed: average spread came back to 6.949, only 5/19 prompts at zero spread. The fix works.

## Third reward-model + RAFT cycle
Retrained the reward model first on the dataset's real growth (211 usable train / 54 val, up from 162) — held-out accuracy landed at 79.63%, a real recovery from the prior cycle's 60.53% low. Ran RAFT against it with the fixed diversity schedule. Manually inspected all 19 winning responses before training, same as every prior round: found 3 broken winners anyway — a stray garbled byte fragment, a nonsensical greeting/farewell blend ("Goodbye! How can I help you?"), and a truncated sentence. Hand-fixed all three rather than dropping the prompts.

Trained via an isolated output directory, live-checked 8 prompts greedily (7/8 identical, the 1 change landing exactly on the hand-fixed response), and verified 0 regressions against a freshly-generated same-code baseline. Promoted.

## A real lead on the Aven-2 parameter mystery
Since Day 3, two runs against the identical Aven-2 config have reported different parameter counts (153,450,496 vs 155,547,648) with no explanation. Did the math: the gap is exactly 2,097,152 — one full embedding table (vocab 2048 × width 1024) — pointing at weight tying.

Checked the actual code rather than stopping at the coincidence: `brain.py` has tied the output projection to the input embedding since its very first commit, and both places that count parameters already deduplicate tied weights correctly (PyTorch's default behavior). The counting logic itself isn't the bug. That means the two runs weren't running identical code despite identical flags — most likely because Aven-2 trains through a separately deployed DirectML venv on the Windows PC that may be out of sync with the committed source. Ruled out the leading wrong hypothesis with real evidence and left a concrete, checkable next step (diff the deployed file's hash) instead of an open guess.
