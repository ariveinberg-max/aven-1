# Build log — Entry 12

The rest of Day 19: a third failed attempt at the labeling bottleneck, and the real mechanism behind why it's happening at all.

## LLM-judge coherence check — a third, honest dead end
After the dictionary-based coherence filter and the adaptive-temperature schedule both failed earlier today, tried the approach both of those investigations pointed toward: an LLM-judge, reusing the exact pattern already proven in this project's regression checker. Live-tested it against the same real garbled and clean examples used for the earlier attempts, this time giving the judge actual prompt context — a real structural advantage the dictionary approach never had.

Result: caught only 1 of 6 real defects — the same rate as the dictionary heuristic — though false positives dropped to zero, a genuine improvement on one axis. Even a proven judge model apparently struggles to catch subtly-fabricated word fragments woven into an otherwise fluent-sounding sentence, even though the same model handles the coarser "is this the right topic" judgment fine elsewhere in this project. Three independent approaches, three real failures, on the same underlying problem.

## The actual mechanism, not just the symptom
That third failure forced a real pivot: if detection-layer fixes keep failing, maybe the problem isn't detectable after generation at all — maybe it's a training-side effect. Earlier this session, a direct comparison had already found that the checkpoint from one RAFT round back (RAFT-2) ties less than the current one (RAFT-3) on identical test prompts. Today, dug into why.

RAFT trains on a single best-of-N winner per prompt each round — pure supervised fine-tuning toward one target, with no negative signal at all. A directly relevant paper found exactly this pattern in practice: standard RAFT-style training without diversity regularization shows "monotonic diversity decline," each round's winner becoming the next round's training data in a real feedback loop. That matches this project's own measurement almost exactly.

The honest caveat, stated at the right confidence level: DPO and KTO's contrastive training (push the winner up, the loser down, rather than pure convergence toward one point) should in principle dampen this effect — but no published research directly tests multiple rounds of DPO/KTO against multiple rounds of RAFT. If this project ever runs that comparison, it would be generating new evidence, not confirming an established result.

## Where this leaves the labeling bottleneck
Three failed technical patches plus one real mechanistic explanation adds up to a genuine change in direction: stop trying to detect or filter around the collapse after the fact, and instead treat "how many RAFT rounds is too many" as the actual lever worth pulling. Concretely: prefer DPO/KTO for iterative improvement over repeatedly re-running RAFT, and treat any future RAFT cycle as a deliberate tradeoff against future labeling diversity, not a free improvement.
