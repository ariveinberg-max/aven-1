# Build log — Entry 2: Day 3 RLHF cycle

## Reward model retrain
Expanded from 88 usable comparisons to 112 (88 train / 24 val) after dedup/conflict filtering — 555 raw logged, only 266 non-tie, and 147 of those were duplicates or conflicting labels. Held-out accuracy: 54.17% initial, climbed noisily (58% → 87% → 83% → 79% → 67%), landed at 75% final — essentially flat versus the prior model's 76.47% despite meaningfully more raw data.

**Honest read:** more labels didn't clearly help here, because most of the growth was duplicates and conflicts, not new signal. Matches this project's own reading-block finding: data quality and dedup matter more than raw volume.

## DPO, added and compared
Built `dpo.py` against the same 112-comparison data — no separate reward model needed. Held-out accuracy converged cleanly to 79.17% by step 100 and held flat through 300, markedly more stable than the reward model's noisy run on the same data. But 3 of 4 live-checked generations were byte-identical to the pre-DPO checkpoint, and the one that changed picked up a minor artifact. Metric stability didn't translate into better real output. Not promoted.

## RAFT run — promoted
Generated 19 candidates from the retrained reward model, from the confirmed-stable step-5499 checkpoint. Manually inspected all 19 before training and caught one broken winner: "Hey there" scored highest only because the reward model picked the least-bad of four weak candidates ("20 times 2 is 12."). Hand-fixed it rather than dropping the prompt.

Trained via an isolated `--init-from`/`--output-dir` path — the trusted checkpoint was never at risk during the run itself. Live-checked 7 prompts afterward: 6 of 7 were byte-identical to the pre-RAFT checkpoint; the one that changed was exactly the intended fix. Ran `compare_capabilities.py` against the frozen v1 suite: 0 regressions. Promoted to `checkpoints/latest.pt`; the pre-RAFT state is preserved at `checkpoints-pre-raft-2026-09-12-backup/`.

## The side effect worth reporting honestly
RAFT's newly confident answers collapsed the preference-labeling tool itself: 29 of the next 35 pair-generation calls came back byte-identical — nothing left to label on the affected prompts. Fixed in two rounds: first, swapping the 6 directly-RAFT-trained prompts for untested phrasings from the same already-safe categories and widening the sampling temperature; when that still tied often in practice, adding server-side retry (up to 6 attempts) with a lightweight plausibility filter, so an identical or obviously broken pair never reaches the person labeling. Verified live: divergence went from roughly 1 in 3 to 5 in 6.

## The one number that actually matters
Reward model accuracy was roughly flat (76% → 75%) across this whole cycle. What changed for real: the live model's output changed exactly once, correctly, on a bug that was actually verified live before and after. Small, honest, and real — not a big number to lead with, and not dressed up as one.
