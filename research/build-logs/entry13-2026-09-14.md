# Build log — Entry 13

A real, deliberate rollback — and an honest accounting of what it actually cost.

## Rolling back from RAFT-3 to RAFT-2
Two things confirmed today made this a real decision, not a guess: RAFT-3's reported reward-model improvement over RAFT-2 (79.63% vs. 60.53%) falls inside the statistical noise band already measured for validation sets this size — it was never actually confirmed as real. Meanwhile, RAFT-3's live generation collapse (the same-response-every-time problem hitting every labeling session) was measured and replicated across two different random seeds. Traded away an improvement that likely wasn't real, to fix a cost that definitely was. Rolled `checkpoints/latest.pt` back to RAFT-2, verified by hash, live-checked clean, frozen suite unchanged.

## Not calling it a clean win
The rollback decision explicitly flagged an unverified assumption: does RAFT-2 actually perform as well as RAFT-3 on the specific prompts RAFT-3's training targeted? Checked it directly rather than leaving it open. Of 19 targeted prompts, 17 came back identical. Two didn't — one is a harmless rephrasing either direction, but one is a real, specific regression: "Can you help me?" now gets a mismatched, confused reply that RAFT-3 had specifically fixed.

That's the honest accounting: this rollback has one concrete cost, not zero. It's a small, isolated one, and a reasonable trade against the diversity restoration — but reporting it as a clean win would have been exactly the kind of thing this project's own discipline exists to prevent.

## Also today: two more real, contradictory experiments
Tested whether repeated DPO training compounds mode collapse the way repeated RAFT does — a genuinely open question from yesterday's research, with no published evidence either way. First run said dramatically yes (8/9 ties vs. 5/9). Replication with a different seed said no at all (4/9 vs. 4/9, identical). Inconsistent — neither confirmed nor refuted, an honest non-result. That inconsistency also forced a re-check of yesterday's own headline RAFT-2-vs-RAFT-3 finding, which — reassuringly — held up in direction across a fresh seed and two temperatures, even though the exact size of the gap varied.

## The throughline
Two single-run comparisons needed replication today to know if they were trustworthy. One held up, one didn't. Neither could have been told apart in advance — which is itself the argument for replicating as standard practice, not a judgment call made case by case.
