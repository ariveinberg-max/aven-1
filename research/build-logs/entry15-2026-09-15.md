## Why the day-of-week bug fix only holds at some temperatures
Yesterday's fine-tune had an unplanned bonus: a long-documented bug ("What day comes after Sunday?" answering "Saturday") started answering correctly at low and moderate sampling temperature. But it reverts to the old wrong answer at temperature 1.0 — a real, reproducible pattern, not a fluke.

Looked into why rather than just noting it. There's a genuine, well-established mechanism behind this: temperature reshapes how *sharp* a model's probability distribution is, not whether it's correct. A factual question like this one has exactly one right answer, so a trained model concentrates its confidence tightly on that single token — a peaked distribution. Raising temperature flattens that peak, pulling weight toward previously-suppressed, wrong-but-plausible answers. Conversational prompts don't have this problem, since many different phrasings are already valid — there's no single peak to flatten in the first place. This is established enough that there's a specific named decoding technique built around exactly this insight ("factual-nucleus sampling," a real NeurIPS 2022 paper).

The honest caveat, stated at the right confidence level: the general mechanism is well-grounded and citable directly. Whether it shows up *worse* specifically because Aven is only 58M parameters is a reasonable, consistent interpretation of what was actually observed — not something separately confirmed in the literature at this exact scale. This also explains, with real grounding instead of guesswork, why this project's whole RAFT-diversity-fix effort (needing high temperature for labeling variety) keeps trading off against factual correctness — it's the same underlying tradeoff, not a separate bug.
